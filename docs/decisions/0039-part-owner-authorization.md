# 0039. A write to a part is a write to its owner

Status: accepted, 2026-10-23 (workstream `ws/sec-part-owner`, finding F3 of the static security scan of `d81d7719`). Contracts 3.2, 3.8, 4.1, 4.9 and 14.2; decisions 0022, 0029 and 0034. Code: `packages/server/src/records/part-owner-access.ts`, `save-pipeline.ts` (`lockOwnersForCreate`, `lockExisting`, `recordChange`), `write-access.ts` and `write-access-rules.ts` (`ownerDecisions`), `packages/api/src/errors/not-allowed.ts`.

Every create, update and delete of a part is authorized against each owner of its chain, up to the aggregate root, with the acting user's ordinary access: they must see the owner and its record rule for `update` must allow on the owner's current state. No owner field is written, so no field rule applies, and a role can be allowed to write parts without being allowed to change any owner field. Both standards STD follows treat parts this way. In TMF621 a note or related party is an item of the TroubleTicket's `note` or `relatedParty` array, not a resource of its own. Both arrays are in the patchable-attributes list of `PATCH /troubleTicket/{id}`, so changing one is a change of the ticket (TMF621 User Guide v5.0.1, PATCH section, pp. 77–78, with JSON Patch samples that add a note on p. 83). TMF630 v5.0.0 Part 1 §4.6 (pp. 95–96) calls such items existentially dependent on their parent, and §4.6.1 (p. 96) says their creates, updates and deletes should go through the parent resource. In ITIL 4 practice tools, work notes and journal entries are part of the incident record, and writing one is an update of that record [unverified: the ITIL practice guides are not available locally]. The shipped helpdesk v3 grants stay as they are: the platform now applies the ticket's assignment restriction to notes and party links. Before this change, an agent could add, change or delete them on a colleague's ticket and advance its aggregate version.

The check runs in the save pipeline, in the caller's own save, after the owner chain is locked and before anything of the part or its aggregate is written. A create checks right after `lockChainForCreate`. An update or a delete checks right after the part's own visibility check, so a hidden part is `NotFound` whatever its owner, and before the version check. An owner the caller cannot see answers exactly as an unknown ID does: `InvalidReference` on the owner reference for a create, `NotFound` for an update or a delete. A hidden owner wins over a refusing one, so the answer never reveals a hidden owner. A visible owner the caller may not update gives `NotAllowed` with the new optional member `refusedOn: "owner"` and its own fixed message. It names no owner, because the part's owner reference may be masked from the caller [judgment]. The refusal comes before any write, and the transaction rolls back, so the part, the owner's version, history and the outbox stay unchanged. Nested saves of record rules and hooks, and after-commit effects, go through the same pipeline with their own identity's access layer. A local rule without `runAs` under the reserved cascade identity is therefore checked as the chain's origin (decision 0034). The system effect `aggregate` allows the owner check, like its other checks: persistence writes only what settling proposed after the caller's own saves passed their owner checks. It can advance the owner's version, but it never stands in for a caller's check. The system user's `WriteAccess.allowAll` also allows, so set loading, service-identity provisioning and data migrations are unchanged. Decisions are made in one statement for all undecided owners of a chain. They are kept per transaction attempt, access layer and owner, and dropped when that owner is saved again in the transaction. A TMF write of N notes to an existing ticket therefore adds one statement for every N (measured: 42, 150 and 1,230 statements before for N = 1, 10 and 100; 43, 151 and 1,231 after). A single `/api/v1` note create adds one (39 → 40 statements, on a ticket that already has one note).

Two edge cases were settled by judgment. First, the parts of an owner that the same acting user created in the same transaction need no decision [judgment]: they are the content of that create (TMF630 §4.6.1), and its own checks authorized it. A TMF `POST` of a ticket with its notes therefore needs only create rights, and adds no statement (42, 150 and 1,230 before and after). An agent who opens a ticket assigned to a colleague can still include notes. Second, an owner's own delete does not cover the deletes of its parts [judgment]. A TMF `DELETE` removes the parts first, while the owner still exists, and each removal is a change to it. Contracts 3.10 already says that a cascade authorizes each affected record as an update or delete by the caller. So deleting an aggregate that has parts needs update and delete on its owner. No shipped role has delete without update on an owner: helpdesk v3 agents cannot delete tickets. Considered and rejected: (a) access-rule conditions that follow the owner reference, which would put the same rule into every part's grants and could not cover rules that admins write later; (b) a pre-authorized cascade delete that lets the owner's delete right cover its parts, which would need a capability the pipeline can verify only when the owner's delete runs. A consequence for Step 4: an approval that is a part of its change needs record-level update on the change. That grant can come with no change-field rights.
